### Phase 3 — Specify Variables, Controls & Instruments

Specify the variable structure as a table: every **independent variable** (the manipulated or grouping factor) with its levels; every **dependent variable** (the measured outcome) with its operational measure and instrument; every **control variable** (the held-constant or covaried factor) with the confound it rules out. **Identify the confounders from a causal DAG.** Draw the directed acyclic graph of the exposure, the outcome, and the candidate covariates, and read the adjustment set off the graph — adjusting for confounders (common causes) while avoiding adjustment for colliders and mediators that would bias the estimate. The DAG is a diagram carried in the design with the metadata header per `rules/visual-leverage.md`; it makes the confounder-control logic auditable rather than ad-hoc. Enumerate the confounds Filter 4 surfaces past the obvious, and pair each with the DAG-justified control that addresses it. Name each instrument — the measurement device, scale, assay, or coding scheme — and record its provenance: a validated instrument cites the source ID that validates it; a new instrument declares the validation it requires before use (R1). The sample frame names the population, the inclusion/exclusion criteria carried from the spec, the recruitment or sampling procedure, and — when the frame touches human or animal subjects, identifiable data, or privacy-sensitive material — the ethics posture, consent, and data-handling plan (R6; the data handling carries the proposal's data management and sharing plan, R8), surfaced as a required inquiry until the operator supplies it.

**Fix the unit, the allocation, and the layout in the design, not in the analysis.** Name the experimental unit and the unit of analysis, and carry both into a preregistered test family that respects the design's blocks, strata, and clusters (R7). State the allocation procedure with its seeded schedule and, when units enrol one after another, the concealment mechanism (R2); state who is masked — participants, those delivering the condition, assessors, the analyst, and the judges of system outputs — or why masking is infeasible (R6); enumerate the nuisance factors, block, randomise, or hold each, and randomise or interleave the run order across conditions; and fix the order structure of a within-unit design — all per `skills/research-suite/references/study-allocation-and-structure.md`. The DAG governs what the analysis adjusts for; the layout prevents the confounds no adjustment can separate.

#### Ablation Design

Specify the **ablation matrix** as an independent-variable factor: disable or remove exactly one component at a time so the delta between the component-present and component-absent levels isolates that one component's marginal contribution (R3). The factor's levels are `component-present` and `component-absent`, and each ablation variant MUST toggle exactly one component — one variant per removed component, never two removals folded into a single run — so the attribution is unambiguous. The ablation matrix is planned here at design time and executed against the frozen frame per `skills/research-suite/references/experiment-program-scaffold.md`.

#### Sensitivity Sweeps

Plan a **sensitivity sweep** for each tunable parameter: sweep the parameter over a principled range and preregister the robustness claim — how the primary metric responds across that range, not a single point (R7). The swept range and the preregistered robustness claim are fixed here before data collection, and the sweep is executed against the frozen frame per `skills/research-suite/references/experiment-program-scaffold.md`. Ablations and sweeps vary one factor at a time and cannot estimate an interaction; where components or parameters are suspected to interact, add a joint layout over the suspect set and preregister the interaction claimed, per `skills/research-suite/references/study-allocation-and-structure.md`. Start each sweep's range from the driver bounds recorded in `proposal.md` §4 (`skills/research-suite/references/plan-branching-and-contingencies.md`), and carry into the preregistration any contingency trigger whose response changes data collection or analysis. A response conditioned on outcome data by arm is specified as a sequential or adaptive design (the Phase 4 stopping rule), never as a contingency.
